\section{Internal Structure of Excitons}
\subsection{Global Phase in Valley-Mixed Exciton}
In this section, we establish the theoretical foundation for further studies of exciton wave packets. A wave packet of the particle refers to a superposition of momentum eigenstates, where the carrier wave (ripples) possesses a characteristic wavelength determined by the dominant momentum component, while the envelope describes the finite spatial extent of the particle. The velocity associated with the envelope, known as the group velocity, corresponds to the particle velocity in classical physics. From this perspective, the envelope of a wave packet provides a good intuition of the particle's position and motion~\cite{griffiths2018introduction, mcintyre2012quantum}.

Using time-dependent perturbation theory~\cite{griffiths2018introduction}, the time-dependent exciton state under weak photoexcitation can be expressed as a superposition of finite-momentum exciton (SFME) states~\cite{peng2022tailoring},
\begin{equation}
|\Psi(t)\rangle
=
|GS\rangle
+
\sum_{S=B\pm}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
|\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle,
\label{exciton_state}
\end{equation}
which represents a linear combination of the ground state $|GS\rangle$ and exciton states $|\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle$ with center-of-mass (COM) momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$. Other notations follow Eq.~(\ref{EXstate}). Within first-order perturbation theory, the amplitude coefficients are given by
\begin{equation}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
=
\tilde{\gamma}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(t)
\left(
\frac{\tilde{M}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}}{\hslash\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\right),
\label{time_coefficient}
\end{equation}
where time--dependent coefficient $\tilde{\gamma}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(t)\approx \tilde{\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)$,
and the natural frequency is approximated as
$\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} = E^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}/\hslash \approx E^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}/\hslash = \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}$. The amplitude coefficients can thus be separated into time--dependent and time--independent parts, where the latter contains the optical matrix element introduced in Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}). For simplicity, we consider excitation by a single LG beam with radial mode index $p=0$, such that the optical matrix element takes the form
\begin{align}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
&\approx
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\ell}(\boldsymbol{Q})
\cdot
\boldsymbol{D}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}*}
\right).
\label{matrixelement2}
\end{align}
Here, derivations analogous to those in the previous chapter---including the rotating-wave approximation and electric dipole approximation---are omitted for brevity. By adopting the polarization unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}$ instead of photon helicity $\sigma$, we relax the restriction to circular polarization. The vector potential is denoted by \add{$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)$, which corresponds to the complex amplitude introduced in Eq.~(\ref{AinCinQ}) but with polarization unit vector not limited to circularly polarized.}

In the work of Guan-Hao Peng \textit{et al.}~\cite{peng2022tailoring}, the envelope function is defined as
\begin{equation}
F^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,t)
\equiv
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}.
\label{wavepacket_envelope}
\end{equation}
To explicitly isolate the static contribution, we use the relation
$F^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,t)
\approx \tilde{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)\, f^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)$,
which defines the static part of envelope function as
\begin{equation}
f^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c},
\label{wavepacket_static}
\end{equation}
where the natural frequency of the exciton state $\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}=E^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\approx E^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}=\omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}$ is assumed to be approximately constant. However, the valley-mixed exciton state obtained from the pseudo-spin model~\cite{peng2022tailoring},
\begin{equation}
|\Psi^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
=
e^{i\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})}
\left(
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
|\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
|\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
\right) / \sqrt{2},
\label{global_phase1}
\end{equation}
naturally carries a $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent global phase $\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})$ arising from its internal structure. Consequently, the optical matrix element acquires the same phase factor,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
=
e^{i\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})}
\left(
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\pm
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
\right) / \sqrt{2}.
\label{global_phase}
\end{equation}
As a result, this $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase can modify the resulting static part of envelope function across momentum space, provided that the internal phase factors $(e^{\pm i \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}})$ partially compensate or reshape the global phase. This observation motivates a more careful examination of the internal structure of the exciton wave function and the explicit extraction of common factors.

\section{\add{Wave functions of finite-momentum excitons}}

To address this issue without resorting to a numerically expensive construction of the exciton wave function, we instead extract its $\boldsymbol{Q}$--dependent phase structure analytically. We begin by considering the internal structure of the exciton wave function~\cite{bajaj2025symmetries, haber2023maximally},
\begin{equation}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{r}_e,\boldsymbol{r}_h)
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{r}_e)
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_h),
\label{excitonWF1}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_e)$ and $\psi_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_h)$ denote the single-particle Bloch wave functions for an electron in the conduction band and a hole in the valence band, respectively. Here, $\boldsymbol{r}_e$ and $\boldsymbol{r}_h$ are the corresponding electron and hole position vectors. The coefficient $\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ represents the amplitude of the electron--hole configuration for exciton band $S$. 

For the purpose of extracting the COM momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase term, it is convenient to rewrite the exciton wave function in terms of a weighted-average position vector $\boldsymbol{R}_c$\footnote{Although this weighted-average position vector is not strictly identical to the conventional COM coordinate in classical mechanics, we retain the subscript ``$c$'' to avoid confusion with the Bravais lattice vector $\boldsymbol{R}$.} and the relative coordinate $\boldsymbol{r}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\boldsymbol{R}_c = a\boldsymbol{r}_e + b\boldsymbol{r}_h,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{r}_e - \boldsymbol{r}_h,
\qquad
a + b = 1,
\\
\boldsymbol{r}_e = \boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{r}_h = \boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Using these relations, the exciton wave function can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r})
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{excitonWF2}
\end{equation}

In addition, Bloch's theorem states that, in a material with a periodic lattice structure---such as a two-dimensional TMD characterized by Bravais lattice vectors $\boldsymbol{R}$---the single-particle wave function satisfies~\cite{griffiths2018introduction, haber2023maximally}
\begin{equation}
\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')
=
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}'},
\end{equation}
where the wave function is expressed as the product of a cell-periodic function, $u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')=u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}'+\boldsymbol{R})$, and a plane-wave component $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}'}$ \footnote{The vector $\boldsymbol{r}'$ denotes a general position vector and may represent the weighted-average, relative, electron, or hole position vector.}. Using this property, the exciton wave function can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r})
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}) \\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
\Big[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})
e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q})\cdot(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})}
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})
e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})\cdot(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})}
\Big] \\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\Big[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})
\Big].\\
&=
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF3}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is obtained by shifting crystal momentum by $-b\boldsymbol{Q}$, which allows the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase originating from the plane-wave components to be written in the compact form $e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}$. \add{Finally, we extract $U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$. Once we eliminate the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependence of $U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$, it should be easier to construct the internal structure of the exciton wave function}

Finally, we adopt the convention
\[
a=1,
\qquad
b=0,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\left[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF4}
\end{equation}

Further simplification can be achieved by performing a Taylor expansion around $\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{0}$ for the implicitly $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent quantities, namely $\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ and $u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)$. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
&=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{m!}
\left(
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\right)^m
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
&&=
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
+
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
+\cdots,
\\[0.5em]
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{m!}
\left(
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\right)^m
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&&=
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
+
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
\label{Taylorindi}
\end{equation}
Their product can therefore be expanded as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&=
\underbrace{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
}_{\text{zeroth order}}
\\
&\quad+
\underbrace{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\right]
+
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\right]
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
}_{\text{first order}}
\\
&\quad+
\underbrace{
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\right]
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\right]
}_{\text{second order}}
\\
&\quad+
\text{higher-order terms}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\section{\add{Zeroth-order approximation}}

Substituting this expansion into Eq.~(\ref{excitonWF4}), the exciton wave function can be decomposed into contributions from different orders of $\boldsymbol{Q}$,
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\begin{aligned}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Big\{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\Big\}
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Big[
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(0)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(2)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+\cdots
\Big]
\\
&\approx
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r}) \quad \rule[0.5ex]{40pt}{0.5pt} \quad \text{zeroth--order},
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF_expanded}
\end{equation}
where the final approximation refers to the zeroth-order approximation \footnote{A similar expansion scheme is commonly employed in evaluating the electron--hole exchange interaction (EHEI) within the Bethe--Salpeter equation, where the first-order contribution is typically retained. Although this may appear inconsistent with the zeroth-order approximation adopted here, the zeroth-order contribution to the EHEI vanishes to leading order, thereby necessitating the inclusion of higher-order terms.} in the small-$\boldsymbol{Q}$ limit. Consequently, the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase factor can be extracted as~\cite{qiu2015nonanalyticity}
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\label{relation}
\end{equation}

In addition, the valley exciton wave functions with zero COM momentum satisfy
\begin{equation}
U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}\,*} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\end{equation}
as derived in appendix~\ref{ch:ZERO}. Therefore, each wave function can be expressed in polar form as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\left|U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right|
\exp\!\Big[i\,\mathrm{Arg}\big(U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\big)\Big],
\\
U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\left| U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right|
\exp\!\Big[i\,\mathrm{Arg}\big(U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\big)\Big].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
From the complex-conjugation relation above, the two wave functions have identical amplitudes while their phases are opposite in sign, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\left|\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right|
=
\left|\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right|,
\qquad
\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]
=
-\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right].
\label{conjugation_relation}
\end{equation}

\section{Exciton Wave Packet for Excitons}
\color{red}
\subsection{General Effect from Exciton Wave Function}

In the work of Guan-Hao Peng \textit{et al.}~\cite{peng2022tailoring}, the envelope function is defined by
\begin{equation}
\tag{\ref{wavepacket_envelope}} %手動將編號設定為引用原本的標籤
F^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,t)
\equiv
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}.
\end{equation}
and its static part is
\begin{equation}
\tag{\ref{wavepacket_static}}
f^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c},
\end{equation}
In this work, we include the internal exciton wave function and define the envelope function more generally as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}, t)
&\equiv
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}), \\
&=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second equality follows from Eq~(\ref{excitonWF3}) and the corresponding static part is
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{my_wavepacket_static}
\end{equation}
$U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol r)$, the internal structure of the exciton wave function, is the additional factor that distinguishes Eq.~(\ref{wavepacket_static}) from Eq.~(\ref{my_wavepacket_static}). In addition, it is both $\boldsymbol{Q}$--dependent and complex-valued. Below, we discuss how this internal structure generally influences the wave packet and the possible sources of its phase contributions.

First, for simplicity we neglect the electron-hole exchange interaction and consider exciton states with well-defined valley degrees of freedom, $K$ or $K'$, to discuss the possible sources of phase contributions. With Eq~(\ref{excitonWF4}), the valley exciton wave function's internal structure is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\left[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The internal structure contains three main contributions :
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Amplitude of the e--h configuration : }&\Lambda^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) \\
\text{Phase associated with electron--hole separation: }&e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}\\
\text{Cell-periodic functions of electron and hole : }&u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\end{aligned}
\vspace{-1em}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Amplitude of the e--h configuration : }
$\Lambda^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ is generally complex-valued and depends on both $\boldsymbol{Q}$ and $\boldsymbol{k}$. Although one can assume it is $\boldsymbol{Q}$-independent as in~\cite{qiu2015nonanalyticity} or apply the zeroth-order approximation used in the previous section, such approximations are only valid in the small-$\boldsymbol{Q}$ limit. This is not generally sufficient for the excitation of excitons by twisted light, which produces a donut-shaped distribution in $\boldsymbol{Q}$ space when carrying non-zero OAM. In other words, the excitation necessarily involves exciton states with finite $\boldsymbol{Q}$.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Phase associated with electron--hole separation: }
The phase factor, $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}$, indicates that the relative position between the electron and hole introduces an additional phase to the wave packet. Interestingly, this means that the exciton may exhibit different wave packet shapes for different electron-hole separations, with two characteristic cases given by
\begin{equation}
\vspace{-0.3em}
\begin{aligned}
\text{radiative recombination : } & |\boldsymbol{r}| = 0 \\
\text{characteristic exciton radius : } & |\boldsymbol{r}| \sim a_{\text{B}}
\end{aligned}
\vspace{-0.3em}
\end{equation}
This connection indicates that the wave packet may reflect the dynamics of the exciton.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Cell-periodic functions of electron and hole : }
The internal structure of the electron and hole's wave function will affect the wave packet envelope. Therefore, obtaining the full wave packet in this case requires knowledge of the corresponding electron and hole wave functions.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Other possible phase contributions (valley-mixed exciton) : }
For a valley-mixed exciton, the wave function is composed of exciton states from both valleys, with a $\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$-dependent relative phase between them. This relative phase can therefore influence the spatial structure of the wave packet. More details are discussed in the following subsection.

Without explicitly calculating the wavefunction, it is still possible to simulate the wavepacket if we neglect these additional phase contributions. In the following subsections, we provide physical considerations for evaluating the validity of this approximation and simulate the wave packet to investigate how the light field influences the exciton wave packet.

\color{black}

\subsection{Valley Excitons}
In the zeroth-order approximation, Eq.~(\ref{relation}) suggests
\begin{equation}
\Psi^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\end{equation}
Accordingly, considering the internal structure of the exciton states, the static part of the envelope function is constructed through Eq~(\ref{my_wavepacket_static}) as
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
\approx
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c},
\label{envelope_valley}
\end{equation}
where the internal structure of the zero COM exciton wave function is given by Eq.~(\ref{excitonWF4}) as
\begin{equation}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Lambda^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{U_function}
\end{equation}
In the following, we introduce physically motivated approximations under which the additional contributions from $U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$ can be neglected, thereby avoiding the need to explicitly calculate the exact exciton wavefunction.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Amplitude of the e--h configuration : }
The momentum dependence of the coefficient
$\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$
can be estimated using the two-dimensional hydrogen model~\cite{parfitt2002twodimensional}.
For the $1s$ exciton state, it is given by
\begin{equation}
\Lambda^{1s,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
=
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}a_{B}^{*}}
{\left[1+\left(\frac{a_{B}^{*}\boldsymbol{k}}{2}\right)^2\right]^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
where the effective Bohr radius is
$a_B^*\approx1\,\mathrm{nm}$.
Since
$\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$
is a purely real function, it does not introduce any additional phase to the wave packet.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Cell-periodic functions of electron and hole : }
We can consider the case where the electron and hole spatially overlap, i.e., $\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{0}$. Under this condition, $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}} = 1$, and therefore this phase factor introduces no additional phase contribution. Beyond simplifying the analysis, this condition corresponds to the configuration required for radiative recombination of excitons.
\vspace{-1em}
\paragraph*{Cell-periodic functions of electron and hole : }
Finally, the cell-periodic functions satisfy
\begin{equation}
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c+\boldsymbol{R}).
\end{equation}
Since the lattice constant (on the order of \AA) is much smaller than the optical beam waist
($W_0\sim1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$),
the spatial variation of the cell-periodic functions is confined within a unit cell and does not significantly affect the overall envelope profile.
Equivalently, neglecting the cell-periodic functions corresponds to describing the envelope function on the scale of at discrete lattice sites, which does not qualitatively alter the resulting wave-packet structure.

\subsection{Valley-mixed Excitons}

For valley--mixed excitons, the corresponding wave functions can be constructed within the pseudo--spin model~\cite{peng2022tailoring} from the valley exciton wave functions. Notably,  zeroth-order approximation (Eq.~(\ref{relation})) remains applicable in this case. One obtains
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Psi^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\end{aligned}
\label{mixed_function}
\end{equation}
Following Eq.~(\ref{my_wavepacket_static}), the static part of envelope function is written as
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
\approx
\frac{1}{ \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{wavepacket_mix}
\end{equation}

Here, $U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$ is retained inside the summation because the $\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$ dependence remains embedded in its internal structure. Using both Eq.~(\ref{global_phase1}) and Eq.~(\ref{global_phase}), the $\boldsymbol{Q}$--dependent terms can be written more explicitly by rewriting the static part of envelope function in terms of the valley indices $K$ and $K'$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
& \hspace{0.75em} \Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})\\
&= \frac{1}{2 \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\pm
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}  \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\left[ e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\pm 
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} U^{K' ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right] \\
&=\frac{1}{2 \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\Bigg[
U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \\
& \hspace{4.8em} \pm
U^{K' ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\label{mixed_phase}
\end{equation}
In this form, the phase of $U^{K/K' ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$, $e^{{+}\!/\!{-}i \mathrm{Arg}\!\left[U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]}$, can no longer be neglected. As a result, a complete construction of the exciton wave function appears, at first sight, unavoidable.

\subsection{Phase in the Internal Exciton Wave Function}

Throughout the above subsections, we assume $U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})=1$ to simplify the simulations. However, the phase of $U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$ may significantly affect the spatial structure of the wave packet. Thus, in the following simulation we investigate the effect of this phase by rewriting Eq~(\ref{my_wavepacket_static}) as
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\bigl|U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\bigr|
e^{i\,\mathrm{Arg}\left[U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]}
\label{wavepacket_mix_2}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Simulations}~\\
For a valley-mixed exciton, the internal wave function contains $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase factors of the form $e^{\pm i \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}$, as shown in Eq~(\ref{mixed_phase}). To investigate how such a phase contribution affects the wave packet, we consider the simplified case
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\bigr| = 1, 
\qquad \text{and} \quad
e^{i\,\mathrm{Arg}\left[U^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]} = e^{i \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{wavepacket_mix_3}
\end{equation}
to investigate whether the phase contribution from the internal wave function affects the wave packet. As a result, Eq~(\ref{wavepacket_mix_2}) becomes
\begin{equation}
{\Phi'}^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
e^{i \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\end{equation}
Here we denote a prime to indicate that the resulting envelope function differs from Eq~(\ref{envelope_valley_B}).

The following simulations demonstrate that
\begin{equation*}
\text{Internal phase alters the central-peak feature of the exciton wave packet.}
\end{equation*}
The central peak is determined by the interplay between the topological charge $\ell$ and the $\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$-dependent phases of the optical matrix element and the internal exciton wave function. This highlights the importance of carefully accounting for the phase of the internal exciton wave function, as it can alter key features of the exciton wave packet, particularly the topological charge $\ell$ at which the central peak occurs.
