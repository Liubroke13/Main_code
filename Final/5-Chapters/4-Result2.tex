\section{Internal Structure of Excitons}
\subsection{Global Phase in Valley-Mixed Exciton}
In this section, we establish the theoretical foundation for further studies of exciton wave packets. A wave packet of the particle refers to a superposition of momentum eigenstates, where the carrier wave (ripples) possesses a characteristic wavelength determined by the dominant momentum component, while the envelope describes the finite spatial extent of the particle. The velocity associated with the envelope, known as the group velocity, corresponds to the particle velocity in classical physics. From this perspective, the envelope of a wave packet provides a good intuition of the particle's position and motion~\cite{griffiths2018introduction, mcintyre2012quantum}.

Using time-dependent perturbation theory~\cite{griffiths2018introduction}, the time-dependent exciton state under weak photoexcitation can be expressed as a superposition of finite-momentum exciton (SFME) states~\cite{peng2022tailoring},
\begin{equation}
|\Psi(t)\rangle
=
|GS\rangle
+
\sum_{S=B\pm}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
|\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle,
\label{exciton_state}
\end{equation}
which represents a linear combination of the ground state $|GS\rangle$ and exciton states $|\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle$ with center-of-mass (COM) momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$. Other notations follow Eq.~(\ref{EXstate}). Within first-order perturbation theory, the amplitude coefficients are given by
\begin{equation}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
=
\tilde{\gamma}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(t)
\left(
\frac{\tilde{M}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}}{\hslash\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\right),
\label{time_coefficient}
\end{equation}
where time--dependent coefficient $\tilde{\gamma}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(t)\approx \tilde{\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)$,
and the natural frequency is approximated as
$\omega^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} = E^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}/\hslash \approx E^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}/\hslash = \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}$. The amplitude coefficients can thus be separated into time--dependent and time--independent parts, where the latter contains the optical matrix element introduced in Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}). For simplicity, we consider excitation by a single LG beam with radial mode index $p=0$, such that the optical matrix element takes the form
\begin{align}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
&\approx
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\ell}(\boldsymbol{Q})
\cdot
\boldsymbol{D}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}*}
\right).
\label{matrixelement2}
\end{align}
Here, derivations analogous to those in the previous chapter---including the rotating-wave approximation and electric dipole approximation---are omitted for brevity. By adopting the polarization unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}$ instead of photon helicity $\sigma$, we relax the restriction to circular polarization. The vector potential is denoted by \add{$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)$, which corresponds to the complex amplitude introduced in Eq.~(\ref{AinCinQ}) but with polarization unit vector not limited to circularly polarized.}

In the work of Guan-Hao Peng \textit{et al.}~\cite{peng2022tailoring}, the envelope function is defined as
\begin{equation}
F^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,t)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}.
\label{wavepacket_envelope}
\end{equation}
To explicitly isolate the static contribution, we use the relation
$F^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,t)
\approx \tilde{\gamma}_{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)\, f^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)$,
which defines the static envelope function as
\begin{equation}
f^{S}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
\frac{1}{\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}} \sqrt{\Omega}}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}.
\label{wavepacket_static}
\end{equation}

However, the valley-mixed exciton state obtained from the pseudo-spin model~\cite{peng2022tailoring},
\begin{equation}
|\Psi^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
=
e^{i\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})}
\left(
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
|\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
|\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
\right) / \sqrt{2},
\label{global_phase1}
\end{equation}
naturally carries a $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent global phase $\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})$ arising from its internal structure. Consequently, the optical matrix element acquires the same phase factor,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
=
e^{i\delta(\boldsymbol{Q})}
\left(
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
\right) / \sqrt{2}.
\label{global_phase}
\end{equation}
As a result, this $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase can modify the resulting static envelope function across momentum space, provided that the internal phase factors $(e^{\pm i \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}})$ partially compensate or reshape the global phase. This observation motivates a more careful examination of the internal structure of the exciton wave function and the explicit extraction of common factors.

\section{\add{Wave functions of finite-momentum excitons}}

To address this issue without resorting to a numerically expensive construction of the exciton wave function, we instead extract its $\boldsymbol{Q}$--dependent phase structure analytically. We begin by considering the internal structure of the exciton wave function~\cite{bajaj2025symmetries, haber2023maximally},
\begin{equation}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{r}_e,\boldsymbol{r}_h)
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{r}_e)
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_h),
\label{excitonWF1}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_e)$ and $\psi_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}_h)$ denote the single-particle Bloch wave functions for an electron in the conduction band and a hole in the valence band, respectively. Here, $\boldsymbol{r}_e$ and $\boldsymbol{r}_h$ are the corresponding electron and hole position vectors. The coefficient $\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ represents the amplitude of the electron--hole configuration for exciton band $S$. 

For the purpose of extracting the COM momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase term, it is convenient to rewrite the exciton wave function in terms of a weighted-average position vector $\boldsymbol{R}_c$\footnote{Although this weighted-average position vector is not strictly identical to the conventional COM coordinate in classical mechanics, we retain the subscript ``$c$'' to avoid confusion with the Bravais lattice vector $\boldsymbol{R}$.} and the relative coordinate $\boldsymbol{r}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\boldsymbol{R}_c = a\boldsymbol{r}_e + b\boldsymbol{r}_h,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{r}_e - \boldsymbol{r}_h,
\qquad
a + b = 1,
\\
\boldsymbol{r}_e = \boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{r}_h = \boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Using these relations, the exciton wave function can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r})
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{excitonWF2}
\end{equation}

In addition, Bloch's theorem states that, in a material with a periodic lattice structure---such as a two-dimensional TMD characterized by Bravais lattice vectors $\boldsymbol{R}$---the single-particle wave function satisfies~\cite{griffiths2018introduction, haber2023maximally}
\begin{equation}
\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')
=
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}'},
\end{equation}
where the wave function is expressed as the product of a cell-periodic function, $u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}')=u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}'+\boldsymbol{R})$, and a plane-wave component $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}'}$ \footnote{The vector $\boldsymbol{r}'$ denotes a general position vector and may represent the weighted-average, relative, electron, or hole position vector.}. Using this property, the exciton wave function can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \\
&\Rightarrow
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
\psi_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c + b\boldsymbol{r})
\psi^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c - a\boldsymbol{r}) \\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
\Big[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})
e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q})\cdot(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})}
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})
e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})\cdot(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})}
\Big] \\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q})
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\Big[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+a\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c+b\boldsymbol{r})
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}-b\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-a\boldsymbol{r})
\Big].\\
&=
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF3}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is obtained by shifting crystal momentum by $-b\boldsymbol{Q}$, which allows the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase originating from the plane-wave components to be written in the compact form $e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}$. \add{Finally, we extract $U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$. Once we eliminate the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependence of $U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$, it should be easier to construct the internal structure of the exciton wave function}

Finally, we adopt the convention
\[
a=1,
\qquad
b=0,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\begin{aligned}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\left[
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF4}
\end{equation}
Under this convention, the whole cell-periodic function becomes independent of $\boldsymbol{Q}$.

Further simplification can be achieved by performing a Taylor expansion around $\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{0}$ for the implicitly $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent quantities, namely $\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ and $u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)$. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
&=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{m!}
\left(
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\right)^m
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
&&=
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
+
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
+\cdots,
\\[0.5em]
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&=
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{m!}
\left(
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\right)^m
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&&=
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
+
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
\label{Taylorindi}
\end{equation}
Their product can therefore be expanded as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
&=
\underbrace{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
}_{\text{zeroth order}}
\\
&\quad+
\underbrace{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\right]
+
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\right]
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
}_{\text{first order}}
\\
&\quad+
\underbrace{
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\right]
\left[
\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\right]
}_{\text{second order}}
\\
&\quad+
\text{higher-order terms}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\section{\add{Zeroth-order approximation}}

Substituting this expansion into Eq.~(\ref{excitonWF4}), the exciton wave function can be decomposed into contributions from different orders of $\boldsymbol{Q}$,
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\begin{aligned}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Big\{
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
\Big\}
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})
\\
&=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Big[
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(0)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}(2)}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
+\cdots
\Big]
\\
&\approx
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r}) \quad \rule[0.5ex]{40pt}{0.5pt} \quad \text{zeroth--order},
\end{aligned}
\label{excitonWF_expanded}
\end{equation}
where the final approximation refers to the zeroth-order approximation \footnote{A similar expansion scheme is commonly employed in evaluating the electron--hole exchange interaction (EHEI) within the Bethe--Salpeter equation, where the first-order contribution is typically retained. Although this may appear inconsistent with the zeroth-order approximation adopted here, the zeroth-order contribution to the EHEI vanishes to leading order, thereby necessitating the inclusion of higher-order terms.} in the small-$\boldsymbol{Q}$ limit. Consequently, the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent phase factor can be extracted as~\cite{qiu2015nonanalyticity}
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\Psi^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{S,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\label{relation}
\end{equation}

In addition, the valley exciton wave functions with zero COM momentum satisfy
\begin{equation}
U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}\,*} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\end{equation}
as derived in appendix~\ref{ch:ZERO}. Therefore, each wave function can be expressed in polar form as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\left|U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right|
\exp\!\Big[i\,\mathrm{Arg}\big(U^{K,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\big)\Big],
\\
U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&=
\left| U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right|
\exp\!\Big[i\,\mathrm{Arg}\big(U^{K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\big)\Big].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
From the complex-conjugation relation above, the two wave functions have identical amplitudes while their phases are opposite in sign, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\left|\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right|
=
\left|\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right|,
\qquad
\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]
=
-\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right].
\label{conjugation_relation}
\end{equation}

\section{Exciton Wave Packet for Excitons}

\subsection{Valley Excitons}
\add{First, for simplicity we disregard the electron-hole exchange interaction and exciton states theat possess well-defoned valley degree of freedom, $K$ or $K'$. In the zeroth-order approximation,} Eq.~(\ref{relation}) suggests
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\Psi^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\end{equation}
Accordingly, considering the internal structure of the exciton states, the envelope function \add{is} constructed as a weighted superposition of exciton momentum eigenstates in real space, with amplitude coefficients derived from first--order time--dependent perturbation theory as
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}, t)
&=
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
\Psi^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \\
&\approx
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c},
\end{aligned}
\label{envelope_valley}
\end{equation}
\add{where} the internal structure of the zero COM exciton wave function is given by Eq.~(\ref{excitonWF4}) as
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
=
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}
(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{U_function}
\end{equation}
At this stage, both the internal exciton wave function
$U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$
and the time-dependent coefficient
$\tilde{c}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)$
introduced through the definition of the envelope function prevent further simplification. 
Moreover, $U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$
contains several components that may introduce additional phase contributions, including the phase factor
$e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}$,
the coefficient
$\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$,
and the cell-periodic functions
$u_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)$
and
$u^{*}_{v,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c-\boldsymbol{r})$.
In the following, we introduce physically motivated approximations under which these additional contributions can be neglected, allowing the phase structure of the wave packet to be primarily determined by the optical matrix elements.

\paragraph*{Time-dependent coefficient}~\\
Following Eq.~(\ref{time_coefficient}), the time-dependent coefficient can be written as
\begin{equation}
\tilde{c}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
=
\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(t)}
{\hslash\omega^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\tilde{M}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\approx
\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)}
{\hslash\omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}}
\tilde{M}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}} .
\label{time_approx}
\end{equation}
In the present analysis, the remaining time-dependent factor
$\tilde{\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{0}}(t)$
is omitted and we focus on the time-independent part, the optical matrix element
$\tilde{M}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}$, which becomes the dominant factor determining the spatial distribution of the wave packet.

\paragraph*{The phase factor $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}$}~\\
We first consider the case where the electron and hole spatially overlap, i.e.,
$\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}$.
Under this condition,
$e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}=1$,
and therefore this phase factor introduces no additional phase contribution.
Beyond simplifying the analysis, this condition corresponds to the configuration required for radiative recombination of excitons.

\paragraph*{The coefficient $\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$}~\\
The momentum dependence of the coefficient
$\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$
can be estimated using the two-dimensional hydrogen model~\cite{parfitt2002twodimensional}.
For the $1s$ exciton state, it is given by
\begin{equation}
\Lambda^{1s,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
=
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}a_{B}^{*}}
{\left[1+\left(\frac{a_{B}^{*}\boldsymbol{k}}{2}\right)^2\right]^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
where the effective Bohr radius is
$a_B^*\approx1\,\mathrm{nm}$.
Since
$\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{0}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$
is a purely real function, it does not introduce any additional phase contribution to the wave packet.

\paragraph*{The cell-periodic functions}~\\
Finally, the cell-periodic functions satisfy
\begin{equation}
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c)
=
u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c+\boldsymbol{R}).
\end{equation}
Since the lattice constant (on the order of \AA) is much smaller than the optical beam waist
($W_0\sim1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$),
the spatial variation of the cell-periodic functions is confined within a unit cell and does not significantly affect the overall envelope profile.
Equivalently, neglecting the cell-periodic functions corresponds to describing the envelope function only at discrete lattice sites, which does not qualitatively alter the resulting wave-packet structure.

With the condition obtained above, and adopting Born's statistical interpretation~\cite{griffiths2018introduction}, the probability density can be estimated from the absolute square of the static envelope function. In particular, for $\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}$,
\begin{equation}
\left| \Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}) \right|^2
\propto
\left|
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\right|^2.
\label{result1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Simulations}~\\
We begin with a brief summary to following simulations. For valley excitons, only the transverse component \add{of light} contributes to the optical matrix element, yielding
\begin{equation} 
\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\approx
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\ell}^{\parallel}(\boldsymbol{Q})
\cdot
\boldsymbol{D}^{X*}_{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\right).
\tag{\ref{matrixelement2}}
\end{equation}
The transverse component of the vector potential in the Coulomb gauge is given in Eq.~(\ref{AinCinQ}). Accordingly, it can be written as
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\ell}^{\parallel}(\boldsymbol{Q})
=
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\parallel}(\boldsymbol{Q}) \hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
\approx
\tilde{F}_{\ell, p=0}(\boldsymbol{Q})
e^{i\ell \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},
\end{equation}
where the approximation follows from the paraxial condition adopted in Eq.~(\ref{approx_to_1}).

\add{Thus,} the \add{$\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent} phase is modified through the dot product \add{between light and} the transition dipole moment \add{and eventually} captures the spatial profile of the exciton probability distribution. The following simulations illustrate the squared magnitude distributions of the 
transverse component of the LG beam, $\left|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\parallel}(\boldsymbol{Q})\right|^2$, 
the optical matrix element, $\left|\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}\right|^2$, 
and the resulting static envelope function, $\left| \Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}) \right|^2$ In this way, we can track how the spatial profile evolves across these stages 
and relate each feature to its underlying physical origin.

For topological charge $\ell = 0$ and $\pm 1$, the Gaussian--like and doughnut--shaped structures (see Figure~\ref{fig:wavepacket1} and Figure~\ref{fig:wavepacket2}) are preserved in both the optical matrix element and the resulting probability distribution (see Figure~\ref{fig:wavepacket_l_zero} and Figure~\ref{fig:wavepacket_l_one}). This behavior directly reflects the spatial structure of the LG beam in angular spectrum space, which exhibits corresponding profiles in real space as well (see Figure~\ref{fig:ffig}).

It is also interesting to look at the case $\Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r} \neq \boldsymbol{0})$. For example, the separation of electron and hole equals to the Bohr radius $(|\boldsymbol{r}|=a_{\text{B}})$. However, the simplification that have been applied on the valley exciton wave functions with zero COM momentum $U^{K/K',\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$ would fail. Meanning the phase factor $e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}$ will become non--zero. Thus one will need to have further numerical calculation if one want to evaluate this condition. 

\subsection{Valley-mixed Excitons}

For valley--mixed excitons, the corresponding wave functions can be constructed within the pseudo--spin model~\cite{peng2022tailoring} from the valley exciton wave functions. Notably, Eq.~(\ref{relation}) remains applicable in this case. One obtains
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Psi^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
&\approx
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\left[
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\Psi^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
B\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\Psi^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\right] \\
&\approx
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\end{aligned}
\label{mixed_function}
\end{equation}
Following Eq.~(\ref{envelope_valley}) and combining it with Eq.~(\ref{mixed_function}), the envelope function can be written as
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}, t)
=
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{c}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}(1)}(t)
e^{-i\omega_{\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}} t}
U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\label{envelope_mixed}
\end{equation}
\add{Similarly, approximate the time--dependent coefficient with Eq.~(\ref{time_approx}) the static part of envelope function is written as}
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
=
\left(\hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}\right)^{-1}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}).
\end{equation}

Here, $U^{B\pm ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})$ is retained inside the summation because the $\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$ dependence remains embedded in its internal structure. Using both Eq.~(\ref{global_phase1}) and Eq.~(\ref{global_phase}), the $\boldsymbol{Q}$--dependent terms can be written more explicitly by rewriting the static envelope function in terms of the valley indices $K$ and $K'$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
& \hspace{0.75em} \Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})\\
&= \left( 2 \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}\right)^{-1}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}}
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}}  \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c}
\left[ e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\pm 
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} U^{K' ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r}) \right] \\
&= \left( 2 \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}\right)^{-1}
\Bigg[
U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \\
& \hspace{4.8em} \pm
U^{K' ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, using the complex--conjugation relation in Eq.~(\ref{conjugation_relation}), the amplitudes of the wave functions can be extracted, yielding
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi^{B\pm}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r})
& = \left( 2 \hslash \omega^{\boldsymbol{0}}\right)^{-1} \left|U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right| \\
& \hspace{3em}
\Bigg[
e^{i\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \\
& \hspace{3em} \pm 
e^{-i\mathrm{Arg}\!\left[U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]}
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left( e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\pm
e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K',\boldsymbol{Q}} \right)
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\label{result2}
\end{equation}
In this form, the phase factors $\left(e^{\pm i \mathrm{Arg}\!\left[U^{K ,\boldsymbol{0}} (\boldsymbol{R}_c,\boldsymbol{r})\right]} \right)$ can no longer be neglected. This is because the summations explicitly depend on the $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent terms $e^{\pm i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}$, which originate from the construction of the valley--mixed exciton states and give rise to nontrivial interference effects in momentum space. As a result, a complete construction of the exciton wave function appears, at first sight, unavoidable. 

As a correspondence to the each terms appearing in Eq.~(\ref{result2}), an artificial phase term could be added through
\begin{equation}
\left| \Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c, \boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}) \right|^2
\propto
\left|
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{i\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_c} e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\right|^2
\label{result3}
\end{equation}

The estimation of whether a peak appears at the center can be obtained as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left| \Phi^{K/K'}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}(\boldsymbol{R}_c=\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{0}) \right|^2
&\propto
\left|
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\tilde{M}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \ell}^{K/K',\boldsymbol{Q}}
e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}}
\right|^2 \\
&\propto
\int_{0}^{\infty} \tilde{F}_{\ell, p=0}(Q)
\left(
\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{i\ell \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} e^{-i\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}} \, d\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}}
\right) dQ \\
&\propto \delta_{\ell,1},
\end{aligned}
\label{result4}
\end{equation}

\noindent
which serves as a reference for the subsequent simulations. For simplicity, we consider LG beams with polarization unit vectors $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}=(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+i\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})/\sqrt{2}$. Similar behavior is observed for other polarization choices. In this case, the Gaussian--like and doughnut--shaped structures are no longer preserved in the resulting static envelope functions (see Figure~\ref{fig:wavepacket_minusQ}). Instead, the profiles are effectively interchanged: a Gaussian--like beam excites a doughnut--shaped distribution, and vice versa. This qualitative change highlights the crucial role of the phase factors in Eq.~(\ref{global_phase1}), as the final summation is highly sensitive to these phase contributions.

Nevertheless, the valley exciton envelope functions obtained in Eq.~(\ref{result1}) still capture essential features and exhibit a clear correspondence to the four sum terms appearing in Eq.~(\ref{result2}). Motivated by this observation, in the remainder of this work we focus on simulating the valley exciton envelope functions and analyzing their characteristic features.
