\section{Theory of Light--Matter Interaction}
\subsection{Fermi's golden rule}

In this section, we examine the interaction between light and matter using \textbf{Fermi's golden rule}. The transition rate for an optical excitation from the ground state $|GS\rangle$, with fully occupied valence bands, to an exciton state $|\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle$ with band index $S$ and center-of-mass (COM) momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$ is given by
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}{\hslash}
\left|
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|\hat{H_I}|GS\rangle
\right|^2
\delta(E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}-\hslash\omega).
\label{Fermi}
\end{equation}
The delta function enforces energy conservation between the exciton energy $E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}$ and the photon energy $\hslash\omega$. Within the pseudospin model~\cite{simbulan2021selective, peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}, the exciton state is written as
\begin{equation}
|\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\hat{c}^\dagger_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\hat{h}^\dagger_{v,-\boldsymbol{k}} 
|GS\rangle.
\label{EXstate}
\end{equation}
Here, the particle operators $\hat{c}_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}^\dagger$ and $\hat{h}_{v,-\boldsymbol{k}}^\dagger$ create an electron in the conduction band with crystal momentum $\boldsymbol{k}$ and a hole in the valence band with crystal momentum $-\boldsymbol{k}$, respectively. The coefficient $\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ represents the amplitude of the electron--hole(e--h) configuration for exciton band $S$, and $\Omega$ is the area of the TMD monolayer.
The matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{Fermi}) can be expressed, within the \textbf{rotating wave approximation}~\cite{griffiths2018introduction}, as
\begin{equation}
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|\hat{H_I}|GS\rangle 
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q} *}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle,
\label{eq:matrix_element}
\end{equation}
where, for weak fields, the \add{time-independent part of} light--matter interaction Hamiltonian is given by~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}
\[
\add{H_I=\frac{|e|}{2m_e}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{r})\cdot\boldsymbol{p}},
\]
with $\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{r})$ the vector potential in the \textbf{Coulomb gauge}, $\boldsymbol{p}$ the momentum operator, and $m_e$ ($e$) the electron mass (charge).
If the vector potential is expanded in a \textbf{plane-wave basis} as in Eq.~(\ref{planwaveinC}), the matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{eq:matrix_element}) becomes
\begin{equation}
\color{G} 
\begin{aligned}
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
&= \frac{|e|}{2m_e} 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} 
\left[ 
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)  \cdot
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|
e^{i\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel \cdot \boldsymbol{r}} 
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}} 
|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
\right] \\
&\approx \frac{|e|}{2m_e} 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} 
\left[
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)  \cdot
\delta_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}}| 
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}} 
|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
\right],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the approximation follows from the \textbf{electric dipole approximation}. In this approximation, the Bloch state wave functions are written as $\psi_{n, \boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})=u_{n, \boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}) e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot \boldsymbol{r}}$, and the plane-wave components combine to yield the Kronecker delta $\delta_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel,\boldsymbol{Q}}$, enforcing the equality between the in-plane wavevector and the exciton COM momentum.

Substituting this result back, the matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{eq:matrix_element})~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced} becomes
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|GS\rangle 
=
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{Q})  \cdot 
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q} *}
\right),
\label{firstmatrixelement}
\end{equation}
where superscript $^*$ denotes complex conjugation, $E_g$ is the band gap at the $K/K'$ valleys and $\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}$ is the exciton transition dipole moment \add{, which is defined as
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
=
\frac{e\hslash}{im_0 E_g\sqrt{\Omega}}\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} \Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\langle\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}|\boldsymbol{p}|\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle 
\end{equation}
}The transition dipole moment obtained from density functional theory combined with the Bethe--Salpeter equation, is given in the small-$\boldsymbol{Q}$ limit by Ref~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}:
\begin{table}[h]
\color{G}
\centering
\caption{Table of transition dipole moments with corresponding exciton types.} 
\label{tab:exciton_dipoles}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5} % 稍微拉開行高，讓分式和向量看起來不擁擠
\begin{tabular}{ll}

\section{Manifestation of Excitonic Optical Transitions with Beating in VVBs}
Under the excitation of VVBs, the corresponding Fermi's golden rule can then be written as~\cite{sanchez2024enhanced}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
=
\frac{2\pi}{\hslash}
\left|
\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
\right|^2
\rho(\hslash\omega=E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}),
\label{transitionrate}
\end{equation}
where the optical matrix element is defined as
\begin{align}
\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
&\equiv
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}*}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\left\langle
\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left|
\frac{|e|}{2m_e}
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}(\boldsymbol{Q};\alpha,\beta)
\cdot
\boldsymbol{p}
\right|
\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}
\right\rangle \nonumber \\
&\approx
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}(\boldsymbol{Q};\alpha,\beta)
\cdot 
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q} *}
\right).
\label{matrixelement}
\end{align}

\noindent
Here we can see that the transition is strongly determined by the inner product of the vector potential and the transition dipole moment, which ultimately involves the beating of the two twisted light beams that determines the weighting of different plane-wave components and thus the structure of the VVB in the $\boldsymbol{Q}$ plane.

\subsection{Optical Matrix Element and Transition Rate for Gray Exciton}
Let us start from the simple case, the GX, whose transition dipole moment is approximately isotropic across the $\boldsymbol{Q}$ plane as shown in Table~\ref{tab:exciton_dipoles}, which means the optical matrix element will reflect the pattern of the light. With Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}), we can derive the time dependent optical matrix elements for GX as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}[b]
\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} \Big( \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^z(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \Big) \\
&\approx \underbrace{\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)}_{\text{\small Envelope}} 
\times \underbrace{e^{i \left( \bar{J} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{\small Carrier}} 
\underbrace{(\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \cdot D^G \hat{\boldsymbol{z}})}_{\text{\small Constant}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_GX}
\end{equation}
\noindent
For the purpose of simulation, the exact optical matrix element for GX without the approximation, $G_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{+}(q_{\parallel}; \beta) \gg G_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{-}(q_{\parallel}; \beta)\approx0$ is obtained as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g D^G}{2i\hslash} \Bigg[& G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \\
 - &\underbrace{iG_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^-(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \sin \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \Bigg] e^{i \left( \bar{J} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_GX}
\end{equation}

On the other hand, transition rate (squared absolute value) is determined by the envelope function:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) 
\propto \left| \tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) \right|^2 \approx \underbrace{\left| \frac{E_g D^G}{2i\hslash} G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right|^2}_{\text{\small Envelope}}
\label{gray_envelope}
\end{equation}
Thus, the node structure of transition rate shown in Figure~\ref{fig:gray_abs} follows the light's pattern :
\begin{equation*}
\text{number of nodes} = |\Delta J| .
\end{equation*}
Interestingly, when number of nodes $\Delta J=\pm1$, we can create a asymmetry pattern in the $\boldsymbol{Q}$ plane thus selectively excite GXs with specific COM momentum. Eventually create GXs that may driven in specific directions.

\subsection{Optical Matrix Element and Transition Rate for Bright Exciton}

Bright excitons, whose transition dipole moments are oriented in the in-plane direction will be excited by the transverse component of the VVBs. With Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}), the alignment between polarization and transition dipole moment is depicted by their inner product within the optical matrix elements as shown below
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} \left( \widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\|}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \right) \\[12pt]
&\approx \frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \times \underbrace{\left\{ \left[ \cos\left( \frac{\Delta\ell}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}} - \frac{\Delta\sigma}{2} \sin\left( \frac{\Delta\ell}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}} \right] \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \right\}}_{\text{Product of polarization and } \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*}} \\[7pt]
& \hspace{25em} \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By explicitly evaluating the dot product, the time-dependent optical matrix elements are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \\
& \approx \underbrace{\frac{E_g D^B}{4i\hslash} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right]}_{\text{Envelope}} \\
&\hspace{25em} \times \underbrace{e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{Carrier}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_upper_BX}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \\
& \approx \underbrace{\frac{-i E_g D^B}{4i\hslash} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) - \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right]}_{\text{Envelope}} \\
&\hspace{25em} \times \underbrace{e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{Carrier}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_lower_BX}
\end{equation}
Here, we can already see that the SAM difference, $\Delta \sigma$, determines the pattern of the envelope function for both BXs' optical matrix elements and further leads to the interaction between the winding of light, $\Delta \ell$, and the winding number of the exciton, $\pm2$, resulting in the effective angular dependence $\Delta\ell\pm2$, which affects the pattern of the transition rate.

\noindent
For the purpose of simulation, the exact optical matrix elements for the upper and lower band BXs without the approximation, $H_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{+}(q_{\parallel}; \beta) \gg H_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{-}(q_{\parallel}; \beta)\approx 0$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g D^B}{4i\hslash} \times \\
& \left\{ H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right. \\
&\quad - i \underbrace{H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{-}(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \left. \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right\} \\[-3pt]
& \hspace{25em}  \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_upper_BX}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{-i E_g D^B}{4i\hslash} \times \\
& \left\{ H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) - \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right. \\
&\quad - i \underbrace{H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{-}(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \left. \left[ -\left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right\} \\[-3pt]
& \hspace{25em}  \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_lower_BX}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Selective Excitation of Excitons}

By summing the contributions in momentum space excited by the VVBs, we obtain the total transition rate, which characterizes the overall absorption strength of the material. Under the paraxial approximation, where $\theta_{\boldsymbol{Q}} = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{Q^2+q_0^2}} \approx \frac{Q}{q_0}$ is taken to simplify the integral, the total transition rate can be evaluated as:
\[
\Gamma^{S}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
\equiv 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\Gamma^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
=
\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^2} 
\int_0^\infty \int_0^{2\pi} 
\Gamma^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
\, Q \, \mathrm{d}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} \, \mathrm{d}Q.
\]
The total transition rates for GX and BXs are obtained as
\begingroup
\color{red} % 將作用域內所有文字設為紅色
\captionsetup{labelfont={color=red}, textfont={color=red}} % 將作用域內的 Caption 設為紅色
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^S(\alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^2} \int_0^\infty \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{2\pi}{\hslash} \left| \widetilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}) \right|^2 \mathrm{d}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} Q \mathrm{d}Q.
\end{equation}
\noindent
With the exact formulation of the optical matrix element (Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_GX}), Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_upper_BX}), and Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_lower_BX})) the total transition rate is obtained as
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{G}}(\alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{\pi \Phi}{2\hslash \Omega} \left[ \frac{E_g D^B}{a_0 q_0 W_0 \hslash} \right]^2 \times \nonumber \\
&\quad\quad\quad \Big\{ \Big[ 1 + \cos^2\big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big) |\ell| + \sin^2\big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big) |\ell'| \Big] + \delta_{\Delta J, 0} \cos\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|} \sin\beta \frac{(\tfrac{|\ell|+|\ell'|}{2} + 1)!}{\sqrt{|\ell|! \, |\ell'|!}} \Big\} 
\label{eq:gamma_G} \\[8pt]
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{B}\pm}(\alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{\pi \Phi}{4\hslash \Omega} \left[ \frac{E_g D^B}{a_0 \hslash} \right]^2 \times \nonumber \\
&\quad\quad\quad \Big[ 1 \pm (\delta_{\Delta\ell, 2} \delta_{\Delta\sigma, -2} + \delta_{\Delta\ell, -2} \delta_{\Delta\sigma, 2}) \cos\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|} \sin\beta \frac{(\tfrac{|\ell|+|\ell'|}{2})!}{\sqrt{|\ell|! \, |\ell'|!}} \Big]
\label{eq:gamma_B_pm}
\end{align}
where the constant radiant power is $\Phi = (a_0 A_0^{\mathrm{LG}} W_0)^2$, and $a_0 = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2c\mu_0}}$. 

\noindent
In addition, considering that the two BXs may not be spectrally distinguishable, the total transition rate for both BXs under the condition can be similarly written as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{B}}(\alpha, \beta) 
=\sum_{S=B\pm} \Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{S}}(\alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{\pi \Phi}{2\hslash \Omega} \left[ \frac{E_g D^B}{a_0 \hslash} \right]^2,
\label{totaltransitionrate_BothBX}
\end{equation}
which is independent of $\alpha$, resulting in a constant total transition rate.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{totaltransition.png}
    \caption{Normalized total transition rates of BXs, total BXs, and GX excited by a VVB with $\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'=1, \ell, -1, -\ell$ , with $\beta=\pi/2$ and varying $\ell$ and $\alpha$.}
    \label{fig:totaltransition}
\end{figure}
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gamma_G}), Eq.~(\ref{eq:gamma_B_pm}), and Eq.~(\ref{totaltransitionrate_BothBX}) explicitly show the selection rules for different BXs and GX, where the condition $\Delta \sigma=-2, \Delta \ell-2=0$ and $\Delta J = 0$ will be satisfied by the same VVB with $\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'=1, -1, -1, 1$. Only under this specific VVB's excitation, the excitation can be switched on and off with the relative phase between the composite LG beams, which can be seen in Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition}. 

For GX total transition rate, the $\ell$($\ell'$)-dependent terms arise in the square brackets and the following factorial term. The former results from the enhancement of longitudinal component, where the light becomes stronger as $\ell$($\ell'$) increases. This effect was reported by Oscar Javier Gomez Sanchez \textit{et al.} in Ref.~\cite{sanchez2024enhanced} and is represented by the $\sin\theta_{\boldsymbol{q}}$ dependence in Eq~(\ref{AinRinQ}). 

\noindent
On the other hand, the condition for coherence is shown as a selection rule with $\delta_{\Delta J, 0}$. When matched, $\alpha$ switches the transition of gray exciton as we have seen in Figure~\ref{fig:gray_abs_alpha}. This selection rule originates from the SOI contribution of the longitudinal component and therefore appears explicitly in the GX transition through the longitudinal field.

\noindent
As for the constant in the front, the beam waist $W_0$, which appears in the denominator of the prefactor for the GX total transition rate, indicates that smaller $W_0$ enhances the longitudinal field component and thus increases the excitation efficiency of GXs.

For BXs total transition rate, the $\ell$($\ell'$)-dependent terms arise only through the combined selection rules. In the example for $\Delta \ell-2=0$, the SAM difference, $\Delta \sigma$, the winding of light, $\ell$, and the winding number of the exciton, $2$, are specifically chosen. This effect is not shown in the VVB formulation (Eq~(\ref{eq:transverse_exact_component})), which has no explicit SOI effect. It is not until the formulation of the optical matrix elements (Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_upper_BX}) and Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_lower_BX})) that we see evidence of these selection rules.

Note that Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition} is simulated under the condition $\Delta \sigma=-2$. If we consider $\Delta \sigma=2$, the selection rule will then select the VVB with the opposite OAM configuration, whose $\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'=-1, 1, 1, -1$ and the conditions, $\Delta \sigma=2, \Delta \ell+2=0$ and $\Delta J = 0$ will be fulfilled. This shows that changing the sign of $\Delta \sigma$ does not change the physical result, since simultaneously switching the OAM and SAM between the composite LG beams gives the corresponding VVB with the same physical selection behavior.

Focus on the VVB which fulfill the selection rule with $\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'=1, -1, -1, 1$, Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition_special} shows the excitation is smoothly switched by $\alpha$.

These observations originate from SOI associated with the longitudinal component of the LG beam, together with matching between SAM difference $\Delta \sigma$, winding of light $\Delta \ell$, and winding number of exciton. The former arises from the gauge transformation, specifically the second term in Eq.~(\ref{LCrelation2}). The latter originates from the correspondence between the winding texture of BXs' transition dipole moment (winding number) and the winding of polarization textures in VVBs. In both cases, Fermi's golden rule provides a theoretical framework for interpreting these results and establishes a basis for further studies involving SOI in the transverse component and their interaction with excitons, or for going beyond the approximation $|\cos\left(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\right)F_{\left|\ell\right|}(q_{\parallel})| \approx |\sin\left(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\right)F_{\left|\ell'\right|}(q_{\parallel})|$.
