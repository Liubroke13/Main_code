\section{Theory of Light--Matter Interaction}
\subsection{Fermi's golden rule}

In this section, we examine the interaction between light and matter using \textbf{Fermi's golden rule}. The transition rate for an optical excitation from the ground state $|GS\rangle$, with fully occupied valence bands, to an exciton state $|\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle$ with band index $S$ and center-of-mass (COM) momentum $\boldsymbol{Q}$ is given by
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}{\hslash}
\left|
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|\hat{H_I}|GS\rangle
\right|^2
\delta(E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}-\hslash\omega).
\label{Fermi}
\end{equation}
The delta function enforces energy conservation between the exciton energy $E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}$ and the photon energy $\hslash\omega$. Within the pseudospin model~\cite{simbulan2021selective, peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}, the exciton state is written as
\begin{equation}
|\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}\rangle
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})
\hat{c}^\dagger_{c,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\hat{h}^\dagger_{v,-\boldsymbol{k}} 
|GS\rangle.
\label{EXstate}
\end{equation}
Here, the particle operators $\hat{c}_{c,\boldsymbol{k}}^\dagger$ and $\hat{h}_{v,-\boldsymbol{k}}^\dagger$ create an electron in the conduction band with crystal momentum $\boldsymbol{k}$ and a hole in the valence band with crystal momentum $-\boldsymbol{k}$, respectively. The coefficient $\Lambda_{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k})$ represents the amplitude of the electron--hole configuration for exciton band $S$, and $\Omega$ is the area of the TMD monolayer.
The matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{Fermi}) can be expressed, within the \textbf{rotating wave approximation}~\cite{griffiths2018introduction}, as
\begin{equation}
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|\hat{H_I}|GS\rangle 
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q} *}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle,
\label{eq:matrix_element}
\end{equation}
where, for weak fields, the \add{time-independent part of} light--matter interaction Hamiltonian is given by~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}
\[
\add{H_I=\frac{|e|}{2m_e}\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{r})\cdot\boldsymbol{p}},
\]
with $\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{r})$ the vector potential in the \textbf{Coulomb gauge}, $\boldsymbol{p}$ the momentum operator, and $m_e$ ($e$) the electron mass (charge).
If the vector potential is expanded in a \textbf{plane-wave basis} as in Eq.~(\ref{planwaveinC}), the matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{eq:matrix_element}) becomes
\begin{equation}
\color{G} 
\begin{aligned}
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
&= \frac{|e|}{2m_e} 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} 
\left[ 
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)  \cdot
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}|
e^{i\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel \cdot \boldsymbol{r}} 
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}} 
|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
\right] \\
&\approx \frac{|e|}{2m_e} 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} 
\left[
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel)  \cdot
\delta_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel,\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\langle\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}}| 
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}} 
|\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
\right],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the approximation follows from the \textbf{electric dipole approximation}. In this approximation, the Bloch state wave functions are written as $\psi_{n, \boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r})=u_{n, \boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{r}) e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot \boldsymbol{r}}$, and the plane-wave components combine to yield the Kronecker delta $\delta_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel,\boldsymbol{Q}}$, enforcing the equality between the in-plane wavevector and the exciton COM momentum.

Substituting this result back, the matrix element in Eq.~(\ref{eq:matrix_element})~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced} becomes
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\langle\Psi^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}|H_I|GS\rangle 
=
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{Q})  \cdot 
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q} *}
\right),
\label{firstmatrixelement}
\end{equation}
where superscript $^*$ denotes complex conjugation, $E_g$ is the band gap at the $K/K'$ valleys and $\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}$ is the exciton transition dipole moment \add{, which is defined as
\begin{equation}
\color{G}
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
=
\frac{e\hslash}{im_0 E_g\sqrt{\Omega}}\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} \Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\langle\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}|\boldsymbol{p}|\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}}\rangle 
\end{equation}
}The transition dipole moment obtained from density functional theory combined with the Bethe--Salpeter equation, is given in the small-$\boldsymbol{Q}$ limit by Ref~\cite{peng2022tailoring, sanchez2024enhanced}:
\begin{table}[h]
\color{G}
\centering
\caption{Table of transition dipole moments with corresponding exciton types.} 
\label{tab:exciton_dipoles}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5} % 稍微拉開行高，讓分式和向量看起來不擁擠
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\hline\hline

\textbf{Valley excitons (bright)} & 
$\begin{aligned}
    \boldsymbol{D}^{K, \boldsymbol{Q}} &= \frac{D^B}{\sqrt{2}} (\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+i\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}), \\

\section{Manifestation of Excitonic Optical Transitions with Beating in VVBs}
Under the excitation of VVBs, the corresponding Fermi's golden rule can then be written as~\cite{sanchez2024enhanced}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
=
\frac{2\pi}{\hslash}
\left|
\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
\right|^2
\rho(\hslash\omega=E^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}),
\label{transitionrate}
\end{equation}
where the optical matrix element is defined as
\begin{align}
\tilde{M}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}
&\equiv
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}} 
\sum_{vc\boldsymbol{k}} 
\Lambda^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}*}(vc\boldsymbol{k}) 
\left\langle
\psi_{c, \boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
\left|
\frac{|e|}{2m_e}
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}(\boldsymbol{Q};\alpha,\beta)
\cdot
\boldsymbol{p}
\right|
\psi_{v, \boldsymbol{k}}
\right\rangle \nonumber \\
&\approx
\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash}
\left(
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'}(\boldsymbol{Q};\alpha,\beta)
\cdot 
\boldsymbol{D}^{S, \boldsymbol{Q} *}
\right).
\label{matrixelement}
\end{align}

\color{red}
\noindent
Here we can see that the transition is strongly determined by the inner product of the vector potential and the transition dipole moment, which ultimately involves the beating of the two twisted light beams that determines the weighting of different plane-wave components and thus the structure of the VVB in the $\boldsymbol{Q}$ plane.

\subsection{Optical Matrix Element and Transition Rate for Gray Exciton}
Let us start from the simple case, the GX, whose transition dipole moment is approximately isotropic across the $\boldsymbol{Q}$ plane as shown in Table~\ref{tab:exciton_dipoles}, which means the optical matrix element will reflect the pattern of the light. With Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}), we can derive the time dependent optical matrix elements for GX as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}[b]
\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} \Big( \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^z(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \Big) 
\\[-1pt] 
&\approx \underbrace{\frac{E_g}{2i\hslash} G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)}_{\text{\small Envelope}} 
\times \underbrace{e^{i \left( \bar{J} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{\small Carrier}} 
\underbrace{(\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \cdot D^G \hat{\boldsymbol{z}})}_{\text{\small Constant}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_GX}
\end{equation}
\noindent
For the purpose of simulation, the exact optical matrix element for GX without the approximation, $G_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{+}(q_{\parallel}; \beta) \gg G_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{-}(q_{\parallel}; \beta)\approx0$ is obtained as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g D^G}{2i\hbar} \Bigg[& G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \\
 - &\underbrace{iG_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^-(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \sin \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \Bigg] e^{i \left( \bar{J} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_GX}
\end{equation}

On the other hand,  transition rate (squared absolute) value is determined by the envelope function:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{G,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) 
\propto \left| \tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) \right|^2 \approx \underbrace{\left| \frac{E_g D^G}{2i\hbar} G_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta) \cos \left( \frac{\Delta J}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{q}_\parallel} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right|^2}_{\text{\small Envelope}}
\label{gray_envelope}
\end{equation}
Thus, the node structure of transition rate shown in Figure~\ref{fig:gray_abs} follows the light's pattern :
\begin{equation*}
\text{number of nodes} = |\Delta J| .
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Optical Matrix Element and Transition Rate for Bright Exciton}

Bright excitons, whose transition dipole moments oriented in the in-plane direction will be excited by the transverse component of the VVBs. With Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement}), the alignment between polarization and transition dipole moment is depict by their inner product within the optical matrix elements as shown below
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g}{2i\hbar} \left( \widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\|}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \right) \\[12pt]
&\approx \frac{E_g}{2i\hbar} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \times \underbrace{\left\{ \left[ \cos\left( \frac{\Delta\ell}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}} - \frac{\Delta\sigma}{2} \sin\left( \frac{\Delta\ell}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \frac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right)\widehat{\boldsymbol{y}} \right] \cdot \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*} \right\}}_{\text{Product of polarization and } \boldsymbol{D}^{B\pm,\boldsymbol{Q}*}} \\[12pt]
& \hspace{25em} \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \frac{\alpha}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Derive the dotproduct explictly, the time dependent optical matrix elements for both BXs are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \\
& \approx \underbrace{\frac{E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right]}_{\text{Envelope}} \\[-4pt]
&\hspace{25em} \times \underbrace{e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{Carrier}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_upper_BX}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-3.8em}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) \\
& \approx \underbrace{\frac{-i E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) - \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right]}_{\text{Envelope}} \\[-4pt]
&\hspace{25em} \times \underbrace{e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}}_{\text{Carrier}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_lower_BX}
\end{equation}
Here, we can already see the sign of spins ($\Delta \sigma$) determine the pattern of the envelope function for both BXs' optical matrix element and further let theinteraction between winding of light ($\Delta \ell$)and winding number of exciton ($\Delta \ell\pm2$) affect the pattern of transition rate.

\noindent
For the purpose of simulation, exact optical matrix elements for upper and lower band BX without the approximation, $H_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{+}(q_{\parallel}; \beta) \gg H_{\left|\ell\right|,\left|\ell'\right|}^{-}(q_{\parallel}; \beta)\approx 0$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} \times \\
& \left\{ H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right. \\
&\quad - i \underbrace{H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{-}(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \left. \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right\} \\[-3pt]
& \hspace{25em}  \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_upper_BX}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}, t; \alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{-i E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} \times \\
& \left\{ H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{+}(Q; \beta) \left[ \left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) - \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \sin\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right. \\
&\quad - i \underbrace{H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^{-}(Q; \beta)}_{\text{Previously neglected}} \left. \left[ -\left(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) + \left(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\right) \cos\left( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \right) \right] \right\} \\[-3pt]
& \hspace{25em}  \times e^{i\left( \bar{\ell}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} - \omega t + \tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{4} (|\ell'| + |\ell|) \right)}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrixelement_exact_lower_BX}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
&\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) 
\propto \left| \widetilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B+,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) \right|^2 \nonumber \\
&\approx \Big| \tfrac{E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta) 
  \Big[ \big(1 - \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\big) \cos\big( \tfrac{\Delta\ell - 2}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \big) + \big(1 + \tfrac{\Delta\sigma}{2}\big) \cos\big( \tfrac{\Delta\ell + 2}{2} \phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} + \tfrac{\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}}{2} \big) \Big] \Big|^2

&\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) 
\propto \left| \widetilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{B-,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}; \alpha, \beta) \right|^2 \nonumber \\
&\approx \Big| \tfrac{-i E_g D^B}{4i\hbar} H_{|\ell|,|\ell'|}^+(Q; \beta)

\label{eq:gamma_B_minus}
\end{align}

\subsection{Selective Excitation of Excitons}

By summing the contributions in momentum space excited by the VVBs, we obtain the total transition rate, which represents the intensity of the material absorption. Under the paraxial approximation, where $\theta_{\boldsymbol{Q}} = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{Q^2+q_0^2}} \approx \frac{Q}{q_0}$ is taken to simplify the integral, the total transition rate can be evaluated as:
\[
\Gamma^{S}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
\equiv 
\sum_{\boldsymbol{Q}} 
\Gamma^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
=
\frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^2} 
\int_0^\infty \int_0^{2\pi} 
\Gamma^{S, \boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma, \ell, \sigma', \ell'} 
\, Q \, \mathrm{d}\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} \, \mathrm{d}Q.
\]
The total transition rates for GX and BXs are obtained as
\color{red}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^S(\alpha, \beta) 
= \frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^2} \int_0^\infty \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{2\pi}{\hbar} \left| \widetilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{S,\boldsymbol{Q}}(\boldsymbol{Q}) \right|^2 d\phi_{\boldsymbol{Q}} Q dQ.
\end{equation}
\noindent
With the exact formulation of the optical transition element (Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_GX}), Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_upper_BX}), and Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_exact_lower_BX})) the total transition rate is obtained as
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{G}}(\alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{\pi \Phi}{2\hbar \Omega} \left[ \frac{E_g D_B^X}{a_0 q_0 W_0 \hbar} \right]^2 \times \nonumber \\
&\quad\quad\quad \Big\{ \Big[ 1 + \cos^2\big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big) |\ell| + \sin^2\big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big) |\ell'| \Big] + \delta_{\Delta J, 0} \cos\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|} \sin\beta \frac{(\tfrac{|\ell|+|\ell'|}{2} + 1)!}{\sqrt{|\ell|! \, |\ell'|!}} \Big\} \\[-4pt]
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,\sigma',\ell'}^{\boldsymbol{B}\pm}(\alpha, \beta) 
&= \frac{\pi \Phi}{4\hbar \Omega} \left[ \frac{E_g D_B^X}{a_0 \hbar} \right]^2 \times \nonumber \\
&\quad\quad\quad \Big[ 1 \pm (\delta_{\Delta\ell, 2} \delta_{\Delta\sigma, -2} + \delta_{\Delta\ell, -2} \delta_{\Delta\sigma, 2}) \cos\alpha_{|\ell|,|\ell'|} \sin\beta \frac{(\tfrac{|\ell|+|\ell'|}{2})!}{\sqrt{|\ell|! \, |\ell'|!}} \Big]
\end{align}
where the constant radiant power is $\Phi = (a_0 A_0^{\mathrm{LG}} W_0)^2$, and $a_0 = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2c\mu_0}}$.

For GX total transition rate, the $\ell$($\ell'$)-dependent arise in square brackets and the folowing factorial term. The former results from the enhencement of longitudinal component, where the light becomes stronger as $\ell$($\ell'$) increase. This effect have been discovered by Oscar Javier Gomez Sanchez \textit{et al.} in Ref.~\cite{sanchez2024enhanced} and is depict by the $\sin\theta_{\boldsymbol{q}}$ dependnece in Eq~(\ref{AinRinQ}). 

On the other hand, the condition for coherence is shown as a selection rules with $\delta_{\Delta J, 0}$. When matched, $\alpha$ switch the transitoin of gray exciton as we have seen in Figure~\ref{fig:gray_abs_alpha}. This selection rule correspond to the SOI nature of the light, thus it is exclusive to longitudinal component.

For BXs total transition rate, the $\ell$($\ell'$)-dependent arise only with multiple selection rules. inculde the interaction batween sign of the spins $\Delta \sigma$. For $\Delta \ell-2=0$, where sign of the spins, $\Delta \sigma$, winding of light, $\ell$, and winding number of exciton, $2$. This selection rules implies except the winding of light and exciton required to be matched, the sign of the spins also play an important role. This effect is not shown in the VVB formulation (Eq~(\ref{eq:transverse_exact_component})), where there is no explicity SOI effect. It is untill the formulation of optical matrix elements (Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_upper_BX}) and Eq~(\ref{eq:matrixelement_lower_BX})) we see a telltale signs of this selection rules is related to the matchment of the polarization and the transition dipole mements, which are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:upper_bright_alpha} and Figure~\ref{fig:lower_bright_alpha}.





From this perspective, the factorial term in Eq.~(\ref{total_transition_rate}) can be interpreted as a geometric correction arising from the mismatch between the radial factors $\tilde{F}_{\ell,p}(Q)$ of the two LG beams. In particular, when $|\ell| = |\ell'|$, it reduces to simple constants, yielding $1$ for BXs and $1 + |\ell|$ for GXs. These values are consistent with the corresponding prefactors in the non-interference contribution (i.e., the term independent of $\delta_{\Delta J, 0}$).

As for the beam waist $W_0$, which appears in the denominator of the prefactor for the GX total transition rate, it reflects that smaller $W_0$ enhances the longitudinal field component and thus increases the excitation efficiency of GXs.

On the other hand, the total transition rate for the total BXs under the condition $(\sigma'=-\sigma)$ can be similarly written as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\sigma,\ell,-\sigma,\ell'}^{B\pm}(\alpha,\beta)
\propto \sum_{S=B\pm} \left|\tilde{M}_{\sigma,\ell,-\sigma,\ell'}^{S}\right|^2 
= \frac{\Phi}{4} \left( \frac{E_g D^B}{a_0\Omega\hslash} \right)^2 .
\label{totaltransitionrate^BothBX}
\end{equation}

Therefore, one can exploit both Eq.~(\ref{total_transition_rate}) and Eq.~(\ref{totaltransitionrate^BothBX}) to analyze the optical transition rates. In particular, we focus on the equator of the Poincar\'e sphere ($\beta=\pi/2$), where the VVB corresponds to a maximal superposition of two LG beams. By varying the azimuthal angle $\alpha$ along the equator, we examine the dependence of the total transition rate on $\ell$ (with $|\ell|=|\ell'|$) using the VVB states $|1,\ell,-1,-\ell;\alpha,\beta=\pi/2\rangle$, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition}.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{totaltransition.pdf}
    \caption{Normalized total transition rates of BXs, total BXs, and GX excited by a VVB state $|1,\ell,-1,-\ell;\alpha,\beta=\pi/2\rangle$, for $\ell \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $\ell \in [-5,5]$, and for azimuthal angles $\alpha = 0, \pi/2, \pi$.}
    \label{fig:totaltransition}

In Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition}, the total transition rate for each exciton exhibits a clear dependence on $\alpha$ when the condition $\Delta J = 0$ is satisfied at $\ell=-1$. As shown within the same column, varying $\alpha$ from $0$ to $\pi/2$ to $\pi$ leads to modulation of the transition rate. This behavior is consistent with the results in Figure~\ref{fig:BXtransition} and Figure~\ref{fig:GXtransition}, where the transition rate exhibits fully constructive or destructive interference over momentum space at $\alpha = 0$ or $\alpha = \pi$.

In contrast, the total transition rate of total BXs becomes independent of $\alpha$ and $\ell$. This is due to the cancellation between the contributions of the upper and lower BX bands, as reflected in Figure~\ref{fig:BXtransition}.

Apart from this special cancellation, BXs generally exhibit independence from $\ell$, unlike GXs. This difference originates from the additional $\boldsymbol{Q}$-dependent prefactor in the GX case, specifically $\sin\theta_{\boldsymbol{Q}} \approx Q/q_0$ in Eq.~(\ref{matrixelement_for_ex}). Consequently, Eq.~(\ref{total_transition_rate}) shows a dependence on $\ell$ even in the absence of interference effects.

Motivated by the special properties of the VVB state at the equator of the Poincar\'e sphere, $|1,-1,-1,1;\alpha,\beta=\pi/2\rangle$, one can achieve smooth and selective excitation over half of the equator ($\alpha = 0$ to $\pi$), as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:totaltransition_special}.

These observations originate from SOI associated with the longitudinal component of the LG beam, together with a polarization-matching mechanism. The former arises from the gauge transformation, specifically the second term in Eq.~(\ref{LCrelation2}), as well as the intrinsic structure of the LG beam. The latter originates from the correspondence between the transition dipole structure of the upper and lower BX bands and the polarization textures of the VVB state. In both cases, Fermi's golden rule provides a theoretical framework for interpreting these results and establishes a basis for further studies involving different VVB configurations, excitonic species, and other comprehensive investigations.
